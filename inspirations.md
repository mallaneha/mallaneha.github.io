# Inspiration for Neha's website

Minimal, clean personal sites of AI engineers, forward deployed engineers (FDEs) and software engineers, with what's worth borrowing from each.

## AI and ML engineers

1. **Andrej Karpathy** – https://karpathy.ai
   A single page: photo, a short bio, then a plain timeline of roles and projects. It shows that a site can be very simple when the work is strong.
2. **Eugene Yan** – https://eugeneyan.com (see /start-here)
   A curated "Start Here" page that groups the best posts by theme, each with a one-line description. Top nav of Writing / Speaking / About, and a dark mode toggle.
3. **Hamel Husain** – https://hamel.dev
   A one-paragraph bio with credentials, then a dated table of posts. It has almost no images. A good model if Neha plans to write.
4. **Jason Liu** – https://jxnl.co (see /resume)
   A resume in a docs layout with bullets led by numbers ("6M+ monthly downloads"), and a bold line near the top saying what he's looking for next.
5. **Lilian Weng** – https://lilianweng.github.io
   A quiet, text-first technical blog with a simple archive and tags. A good reference for readable long-form posts.
6. **Chip Huyen** – https://huyenchip.com
   Short intro, then links to her books, blog and talks. Warm first-person tone while still reading as professional.
7. **Simon Willison** – https://simonwillison.net
   A dense but plain blog. Worth borrowing the short "TIL"/link-post format for frequent small updates.

## Software engineers (design-forward minimal)

8. **Brittany Chiang** – https://brittanychiang.com
   The most-copied engineer portfolio. One page with an About / Experience / Projects anchor nav and a fixed left column. Skill tags on each job, metric badges on project cards, and a colophon footer.
9. **Lee Robinson** – https://leerob.com
   Very sparse. A short paragraph intro, a few links, writing. Shows how far restraint plus good type can go.
10. **Paco Coursey** – https://paco.me
    Small type, lots of white space, projects as a simple list with years. Minimal without feeling empty.
11. **Rauno Freiberg** – https://rauno.me
    Minimal on the surface with small, polished interactions. Good if Neha wants one memorable detail.
12. **Emil Kowalski** – https://emilkowal.ski
    Clean single column with careful animation and a "craft" section. Shows that motion works best when there's very little of it.
13. **Julia Evans** – https://jvns.ca
    Plain, friendly and personal. Borrow the honest, curious tone.
14. **Emilia Psacharopoulos** – https://emiliapsacharopoulos.github.io
    Project and experience entries show a short version by default, with an optional long version. Her write-up on rebuilding the site (/notes/2026-03-22-rebuilding-personal-website) explains the approach.

## Forward deployed engineers

I couldn't find FDE personal sites that are truly minimal. The ones that show up (szymonpaluch.com, rohitghumare.com, heyarnoux.com) are consultant sites with a lot of promo blocks. Two details are still worth borrowing:
- **Rohit Ghumare** – https://rohitghumare.com: a restrained article layout with breadcrumbs, numbered figures and "Keep reading" cards.
- **David Arnoux** – https://www.heyarnoux.com: a row of big stat callouts under the intro and simple box-and-arrow diagrams of how he works. For an FDE these could show "customer problem → what I built → result".

## Common patterns

- **One column at reading width**, or one page with anchor links. Few sites have more than 3–5 nav items (usually About, Experience/Work, Projects, Writing, Contact).
- **The intro does the work**: a name, a one-line role ("AI engineer at X, building Y"), and 2–3 sentences in first person. A photo is optional.
- **Proof over adjectives**: numbers in bullets (users, latency, revenue, downloads), dated entries, links to real artifacts (repos, demos, talks).
- **Typography carries the design**: one sans-serif (Inter, Geist, or a system font), sometimes with a serif for headings. Neutral palette with one accent color, and dark mode.
- **Writing as a dated list or table**, not a card grid.
- **A small personal touch**: a colophon, a "now" line, a playful footer, or one subtle animation.
- **For an FDE angle specifically**: short customer case studies (problem, what was deployed, outcome) and a clear "what I'm looking for" line, which jxnl.co does well.
